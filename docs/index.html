Gedanken

Run the thought experiment before you risk the capital.

Gedanken tests market research in thousands of simulated markets where the right answer is planted in advance. It finds out which methods find real edges and which ones fool themselves, then rebuilds its own methods under a judge it cannot change. It does two jobs: it generates and tests alpha ideas, and it produces economic research on how shocks move through an economy. The engine is general; each market gets a domain pack built from its own causal story. Three packs run today: stock factors, the chain from natural gas to fertilizer producers' earnings, and energy-to-food pass-through. Power and AI data centers come next.

EXPERIMENT 01 · STOCK-FACTOR PACK

Can your factor research tell alpha from luck?

We generated 300 stock markets (200 stocks, 10 years of monthly returns, fundamentals reported one to two months after each quarter) with no edge at all, and 300 where value and quality carry a real, planted premium.

A typical search tries 76 factor variants and keeps the best. In markets with no edge, it claims one 99% of the time. Two mistakes compound: trying many variants, and stamping fundamentals at quarter end, before investors could see them.

The disciplined test commits to one hypothesis, on data as investors saw it. It claims a false edge in 4% of those markets and still finds 90% of the real ones.

EXPERIMENT 02 · GAS-TO-FERTILIZER PACK

When everyone asks the same AI, follow it or fade it?

More investors now ask LLMs for views. Research shows that early on, LLM reads of the news predicted returns, and that the edge shrank as adoption grew. Separate work finds LLM-guided retail trading moves together and then reverses.

Gedanken simulates that crowd. Slide adoption up and watch following the LLM stop paying while fading it starts to pay. A fundamental signal, hedged when its view matches a crowded LLM view, holds up throughout.

The crossover depends on assumptions about how much the crowd moves prices, so the real level has to come from live data. That is what the daily consensus log is collecting.

EXPERIMENT 03 · GAS-TO-FERTILIZER PACK

A research system that improves itself and cannot grade its own homework

A proposer, the Architect, suggests one change at a time to the research method. It can be a built-in library of edits or an LLM; the runs shown here used the built-in library. Sentinel, a fixed judge, scores each change on 1,000 fresh markets with no edge and 400 with one, and accepts it only if false discoveries stay under 5% and the gain is significant. The bar rises with every attempt, so tinkering gets harder.

Sentinel's first version had a little slack. The loop found it immediately and accepted the one change that spent it. Sentinel was tightened, by a human, and the loop then found a real improvement: sizing positions by signal strength raised detection from 25% to 33% with 2% false discoveries.

Sentinel's code is hash-locked. The loop refuses to run if it changes without a human signature.

EXPERIMENT 04 · GAS-TO-FERTILIZER PACK

Déjà Vu: noticing when the real world leaves the simulation

Simulations are only useful while they resemble the market. Déjà Vu fingerprints the market every two weeks and measures how far it sits from the simulated worlds. To test it, we built 22 years of synthetic market data with a hidden structural break: from August 2021, European gas decouples from US gas and spikes, the way it did in the real 2021 to 2022 crisis.

EXPERIMENT 05 · STOCK-FACTOR PACK · ALPHA GENERATION

Can Gedanken find alpha on its own?

Telling alpha from luck is half the job. The other half is finding it. We gave Gedanken's alpha generators 300 fresh stock markets with planted edges and 300 with none. Each generator chose its ideas on the first 10 years under one rule, a bar that rises with every idea tried, and those ideas then ran untouched for the next 10 years on data that was knowable at the time.

An AI economist writes its hypotheses down before seeing any data: the mechanism, the expected direction and the result that would refute it. A wider search explores within the fields and signs it allows. A point-in-time search tries every field that was knowable on its date. Brute force also uses fundamentals stamped at quarter end, before investors could see them.

The economist's most valuable contribution was a rule, not an idea: it refused fields that were not knowable on their date, and those are the fields that gave brute force false alpha in 40% of markets with no edge. Its specific ideas paid off only when the textbook matched the market. The most robust generator was the wide point-in-time search, which kept about half of the planted alpha in both kinds of market with 3% false alpha.

Switch between markets where the textbook factors carry the edge and markets where the edge sits on other fields, with the opposite sign. Alpha captured is the share of a perfect-foresight strategy's Sharpe ratio kept in the untouched 10 years. The economist alone ran slightly above the 5% gate (6.3% to 7.0% over two runs), which is flagged for the next version of the judge.

EXPERIMENT 06 · ENERGY-TO-FOOD PACK · ECONOMIC RESEARCH

When natural gas jumps, how much reaches the grocery bill, and how fast?

Not every question is about trading. The energy-to-food pack follows a gas price shock through nitrogen fertilizer, farm input costs, crop prices and the food-at-home price index. Before touching real data, Gedanken built 400 simulated economies with planted pass-through, each 25 years of monthly data, plus economies where the links are cut, and tested its method against them.

The test changed the method. With one common choice of standard errors, it raised false alarms 14.3% of the time at 24 months in economies with no pass-through. The choice it kept held false alarms to 4.8% at 3 months and 7.9% at 24, with 95% intervals that covered the planted truth 93% to 96% of the time.

The validation also set the limits. The method catches the gas effect on fertilizer every time at 3 months, catches a real but small effect on grocery prices less than half the time, and cannot separate a gas effect on crop prices from weather and demand. Those limits become rules for what any published finding may claim. The real-data run comes next and stays internal until it is reviewed.
